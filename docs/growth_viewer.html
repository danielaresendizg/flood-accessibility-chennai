Chennai: a city severed from its waterways

Historically shaped by colonial expansion, Chennai's urban growth evolved along its intricate network of rivers — the Cooum, Adyar, and Kosasthalaiyar. From an initial footprint of 1.6 km² in 1633, the city expanded dramatically to over 328 km² by 2006, progressively altering natural drainage patterns and intensifying flood vulnerability.

The severance of the once-symbiotic relationship between the urban fabric and its waterways has diminished the city's natural flood absorption capacity, transforming former floodplains and canal corridors into impermeable, fragmented landscapes increasingly susceptible to hydroclimatic extremes.
